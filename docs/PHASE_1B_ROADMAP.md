# Proposed Phase 1B

## 1. Review and validate the teaching

Ask a chess educator to review all authored positions and language. Run small beginner usability sessions observing whether learners can name threats and explain why their chosen square is safe. Include touch, keyboard, and screen-reader access. Change misleading wording before expanding content.

Deliverable: reviewed content ledger, observed usability issues, and a clear beginner model of “attacked,” “defended,” and “safe.”

## 2. Add varied practice and delayed transfer

Author a small bank varying piece types, colors, orientation, distance, blockers, and distractors. Add genuinely new transfer positions and a delayed recheck. Separate initial exposure, repeated-position practice, and unseen transfer in stored evidence. Keep deterministic content and validate all alternatives.

Deliverable: fresh and delayed assessments that cannot be passed by remembering this lesson’s squares.

## 3. Introduce a modest learner model

Use first-attempt accuracy, hint use, error patterns, and retention to suggest the next practice. Begin with explicit, inspectable rules, not an opaque score. Route pawn-direction errors to pawn attacks; missed long-range threats to line tracing. Keep “needs more evidence” as a real outcome.

Deliverable: a small local skill-evidence model and explainable exercise selection. Establish mastery thresholds only after observing actual learners; include uncertainty and retention.

## 4. Teach the boundary of piece safety

Follow with attacked versus defended pieces, favorable exchanges, and checking forcing replies. Explain why the Phase 1A no-attacks objective is useful but incomplete. Add bounded multi-ply exercises and reviewed continuations when the learning goal requires them.

Deliverable: a second complete lesson with explicit concepts and transfer tests, rather than many shallow topics.

## 5. Test learning claims

Define a pilot protocol: baseline and unseen post-tests, delayed retest, comparable practice time, and a suitable alternative method. Measure reasoning and avoidable piece losses in real games as well as test accuracy. Decide what improvement would be meaningful before collecting data.

Deliverable: an evaluation plan and appropriately qualified results. Do not describe the platform as 10× more effective without credible evidence.

## Engineering follow-up

Add storage export/reset and a content-version migration policy if learners rely on local history. Extend browser coverage to WebKit/Firefox and physical phones. Update the ESLint toolchain once all plugins support the newer major. Consider a database, account synchronization, engine analysis, or AI only for a specific approved need after this learning loop has been evaluated.
