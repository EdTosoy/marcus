# Product vision

Quiet Chess helps beginners build habits they can use without a tutor: notice threats, recognize patterns, calculate candidate moves, explain choices, and learn from mistakes.

The long-term aspiration is dramatically more efficient learning, potentially 10× conventional methods. That is a hypothesis to test, not a promise or a measured outcome.

## Principles

1. Understanding before memorized moves. Ask for a decision and connect feedback to the position.
2. Practice before passive consumption. Demonstrate an idea, then require the learner to apply it.
3. Mastery before completion. Keep evidence of hints, retries, and transfer; never award mastery for finishing a screen sequence.
4. Personal improvement before a generic ladder. Eventually select practice from demonstrated needs and retention.
5. Real chess skill before engagement. Time on site, streaks, and lesson counts are not improvement metrics.
6. Patient guidance. Start with a conceptual hint; do not reveal a correct destination after a mistake.
7. Reviewed, deterministic content. Explanations and feedback must have verifiable chess grounding.
8. A phone is a first-class learning environment. Keep the board readable and the current instruction close.

## Current slice

One beginner lesson teaches the habit: **find the threat, choose a destination, check the destination**. It includes a visual example, identification, independent movement decisions, graduated hints, retries, reasoning, and a distinct transfer position.

The practice board is a small supporting tool for rules exploration and FEN loading. It is not an analysis dashboard. No server-side learner account, chess engine, AI service, or remote dependency is needed at runtime.

## Evidence we eventually need

Evaluate unseen-position accuracy, delayed retention, reasoning quality, and fewer avoidable piece losses in real games. Compare against a defined alternative using similar learner levels and practice budgets. Distinguish immediate improvements from durable skill, repeated-position familiarity, and testing effects.

Automated tests establish software behavior and content invariants. They do not establish pedagogical effectiveness, measured mastery, or a 10× efficiency gain.
