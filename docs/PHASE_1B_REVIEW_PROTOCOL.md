# Phase 1B educator and learner review protocol

Status: prepared, not executed. Automated validation establishes chess-rule consistency, not teaching quality, retention, or improved game play. Do not describe the app as validated mastery training or 10× more effective.

## Educator review

Ask a chess educator to inspect the original lesson and all 35 exercises in `src/domain/curriculum/foundations.ts`. Review the displayed orientation, marked target, instruction, every accepted alternative, distractors, conceptual hints, and correct/incorrect explanations. Record reviewer, content version, exercise ID, finding, severity, proposed wording, and resolution in a local review ledger. No reviewer approval has yet been collected.

Check pawn direction, line blockers, knight jumps, both colors, and multiple attackers. Confirm that geometric defenders and attacks by pinned pieces are explained accurately. Confirm the narrow hanging/no-attacks definitions and that immediate capture questions do not imply forced material loss. Assess whether reserved positions vary reasoning sufficiently; reflection/color-reversal checks do not establish independent educational transfer.

Treat a wrong chess claim, misleading accepted/rejected answer, exposed assessment solution, or impossible instruction as a release blocker. Revalidate changed content mechanically, version persisted content when its interpretation changes, and repeat affected browser journeys.

## Beginner observation

Use a small formative sample of beginners spanning phone touch and desktop keyboard use, including assistive technology where feasible. Explain local storage and the optional JSON export; obtain consent before collecting observations. Use anonymous participant codes in separate study notes, and collect no identifying information in app data.

1. Start with a fresh browser profile and the five-decision baseline. Avoid teaching, think-aloud prompts, or coaching during sealed checks; ask about reasoning afterward without revealing future reserved positions.
2. During guided practice, observe whether the learner can name the attack, trace blockers, distinguish defenders, and explain a destination or opponent reply before answering. Record hesitation, wording confusion, guessing, hints, reflection choices, and accidental selections. Do not infer a misconception from a reflection button alone.
3. Observe unfamiliar transfer without assistance. Distinguish first-answer success from eventual guided success and record the time spent practicing.
4. Return after the actual scheduled interval, using the same local profile and a reliable device clock. Do not simulate elapsed time in a learner study. Record independent delayed responses and whether the learner can apply the idea to a genuinely new board.
5. Check phone readability, board orientation, scrolling, keyboard focus, reduced motion, feedback announcements, reload recovery, and export comprehension. Record device/browser and task-specific barriers. Browser emulation alone does not satisfy this step.

## Learning-effectiveness pilot

Before an efficacy claim, define the target learner population, sample size rationale, comparable practice time, an appropriate alternative learning method, unfamiliar pre/post assessments, delayed retest interval, and a meaningful improvement threshold. Keep held-out content unavailable during teaching. Include explanation quality and avoidable piece losses in real games, rather than app completion alone. Prespecify how hints, missing follow-ups, prior chess exposure, and repeated positions affect analysis.

The current bank is small and the thresholds are provisional. Extend and independently review assessment content before a study that requires more unseen checks. Report uncertainty and unsuccessful outcomes alongside successes; do not generalize from a small formative usability sample to chess strength or learning efficiency.
